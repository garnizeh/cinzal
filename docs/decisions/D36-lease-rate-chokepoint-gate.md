# D36 — The lease-rate exit criterion can't be met by a `Config` edit: loosen the chokepoint gate, or recalibrate the band?

**Status:** decided
**Blocks:** M2's exit-criteria closure for the lease-rate row (GDD §22); any future `LeaseCostPerBlock`/`LeaseBlockRounds` retuning task
**Decided:** 2026-08-20
**Issue:** [#231](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/231)

## The question

[#204](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/204)'s exit demonstration swept `LeaseCostPerBlock` (0–20) × `LeaseBlockRounds` (1–15) at 2/3/4/5 players, both bot tiers, 10,000 matches per configuration (106 configurations total, written up in `docs/exit-demos/204-lease-rate.md`). Every measured point stays at least an order of magnitude below GDD §22's `< 1` failing floor, let alone the 2–4/player target band. No `Config` edit closes this gap: the root cause `#204` identified is `OperatorOptions.ChokepointTrafficRate` (0.98) / `ChokepointMinObserved` (9) (`internal/bots/operator.go`) — a bots-package tuning pair outside `game.Config` and outside anything `cmd/simulate --sweep` can reach — which makes Operator attempt a stake so rarely that the economic dial has almost nothing to act on.

That gate was already a deliberate choice, made before `#204`, justified against a different metric: `operator.go`'s own comment states every looser threshold "measurably cost Operator mean RP against Runner." `#231` is the one that conflicts with `#204`'s finding.

## Why it is open

- **Loosening the gate to hit the lease-rate band probably costs RP performance again** — the same trade-off that set it high in the first place, now read in the other direction. `#231` itself states this isn't known without re-measuring.
- **The 2–4/player band may itself be uncalibrated against what Operator actually models.** [D35](D35-simulation-sample-size-and-verdict-rule.md)'s own tier table reads the lease rate against Operator because leasing is "a strategic choice" — but Operator's specific strategy (heat-map chokepoint reading) is a narrower, more conservative model of "buying a post" than a human's, and GDD §10.1's own worked example already frames a post's payoff as "clearly a loss on points... you're buying the vision and the sector majority, not the two points."
- **A secondary, distinct mechanism also caps the low end regardless of the gate's threshold:** `LeaseBlockRounds=1` gives Operator exactly zero live leases at final scoring, at every player count and every cost tested, because a one-round lease always expires before `maybeRenewLease`'s "about to lapse" check gets a second look at it (`internal/bots/runner.go:471-494`). Doesn't affect the shipped default (`LeaseBlockRounds: 3`); tracked separately as [#236](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/236), not part of this decision.

## Options

**A — Loosen `ChokepointTrafficRate`/`ChokepointMinObserved`, re-measure the RP-performance cost.** Directly addresses the lease-rate band, if the band is reachable at some point on that dial. Risk stated in the issue: re-opens the RP-performance question the current values were tuned to close, and needs its own sweep to quantify the cost before committing.

**B — Recalibrate GDD §22's 2–4/player target against Operator's actual (conservative) leasing model**, treating the measured range as the answer rather than a gap to close. Cheapest option; risk stated in the issue: quietly validates an overly cautious Operator rather than checking whether the caution itself is warranted.

**C — Leave both as they are, and let M5.5's human playtesting be the actual read on the lease rate**, per `#204`'s own carried-forward caveat that Operator's chokepoint gate is "a narrower instruction than what a human does with a post." Defers the decision instead of making it; leaves an M2 exit criterion formally unmet in the meantime, per the issue's own framing.

## Decision

**C**, on the strength of a direct measurement of Option A that the issue asked for but did not yet have.

Option A is closed empirically, not just judged too risky to attempt. Option B's premise — that the measured range reflects a calibrated, if conservative, read of "a rational actor's leasing frequency" that GDD §22 should simply adopt — turns out not to survive the same measurement. Both are decided by a paired-cohort sweep run against this decision (see Reasoning), at D35's own 10,000-match/interval bar since the lease rate is one of D35's governed rows — the pairing itself (same seed set drives both cohorts) follows the precedent `internal/rules/bots_operator_golden_external_test.go`'s `TestOperatorBeatsRunnerOverAThousandMatches` already established for reading Operator's RP margin against Runner, though that test's own smaller sample (1,000 matches, no formal interval) answers a different question — "does Operator beat Runner at all" as a one-off acceptance check — not a §22 exit-criteria measurement.

## Reasoning

**Measurement, at D35's own bar.** Since this decision governs one of D35's seven §22 exit-criteria rows (the lease rate), the decisive points are measured at D35's stated rigor — 10,000 matches per configuration, `mean ± 1.96·s/√n` over the per-match vector — not a smaller convenience sample. Golden root `0xa0...`, 4 players, `game.DefaultConfig()`, git SHA `22ff49f`. Runner and each Operator cohort are driven by the same seed-per-index scheme (`seed[31]=byte(i)`, `seed[30]=byte(i>>8)`, `i` in `0..9999`), so the two are paired by construction; the RP margin below is the mean of the 10,000 per-match `OperatorRP − RunnerRP` differences and its own `± 1.96·s/√n` interval — not a comparison of two independent intervals — for the same reason D35 pairs the confrontation-load and lease-rate sweeps on a shared seed set: it cancels map/contract-pool variance out of the margin instead of letting it swamp the signal. Runner baseline: mean RP 1.8241 [1.8029, 1.8453] (n=10,000).

| `ChokepointTrafficRate`, `ChokepointMinObserved` | Operator mean RP [95% CI] | Paired margin vs. Runner [95% CI] | Mean leases/player at scoring [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.98, 9 (shipped default) | 1.8351 [1.8138, 1.8564] | +0.0110 [+0.0003, +0.0218] | 0.00175 [0.00134, 0.00216] |
| 0.0, 0 (gate off entirely) | 1.6030 [1.5828, 1.6233] | −0.2210 [−0.2416, −0.2005] | 0.28387 [0.27853, 0.28922] |

`0, 0` is the most permissive setting expressible in `OperatorOptions` — `findChokepoint`'s `ObservedRounds <= 0` check is unconditional and cannot be loosened further, so this is the true ceiling the mechanism can reach through this dial, not an arbitrarily chosen extreme.

**Scope: 4 players only, not the full 2/3/4/5 D35 normally runs.** `#204`'s own sweep already found the lease rate undershoots by the same order of magnitude at every player count with no player-count-dependent mechanism in play (the chokepoint gate and the contract-priority ordering both operate identically regardless of player count), and its own 2D cross-check set the same precedent — "run at 4 players / Operator only... the compute would be better spent on whatever follow-up the root-cause finding leads to" — for a targeted question that doesn't need the full grid to answer. This decision's question is narrower still: whether *any* point on this specific two-field dial, at 4 players, can reach the band at an acceptable cost. A negative answer at 4 players, on the dial's own most permissive setting, isn't strengthened by repeating it at four player counts instead of one.

**Option A is closed at both tested endpoints, not merely judged expensive.** Even with the chokepoint gate removed outright — its own most permissive setting, since `findChokepoint`'s `ObservedRounds <= 0` check cannot be loosened past `(0, 0)` — the lease-rate interval — [0.27853, 0.28922] — sits entirely below GDD §22's `< 1` *failing* floor (let alone the 2–4 target band, 7–14× further out), at a cost interval — [−0.2416, −0.2005] — that sits entirely on the losing side of zero: a real, statistically clear RP cost, not a wash. This closes Option A at the two configurations actually measured; it is not a proof that every intermediate `(ChokepointTrafficRate, ChokepointMinObserved)` pair between them is bounded by these two. The set of qualifying nodes is a strict superset at `(0, 0)`, but `findChokepoint` picks the *nearest* qualifying node, so a larger candidate set can change *which* node a round targets, not only *whether* one is targeted at all — match-level lease count is plausible, but not formally proven, to be monotonic in the threshold. `#231`'s own framing — "loosening the gate probably costs RP performance again... how much, and whether that's acceptable, isn't known without re-measuring" — is now answered with an interval at the setting the option actually means (fully loosened): the cost is real and even the maximal setting doesn't buy the outcome.

**The shipped default's own margin turns out thinner than the original root-cause framing implied.** At proper sample size, Operator's edge over Runner at the shipped gate is +0.0110 [+0.0003, +0.0218] — real (the interval clears zero, if barely) but far smaller than a quick look at the numbers might suggest, and nowhere near the >4× swing a naive comparison of point estimates across the two rows would read. This doesn't change the decision: the gate-off case is unambiguous regardless of how thin the default's own margin is, and the qualitative conclusion (neither tested endpoint reaches the band, and the permissive one costs real RP) holds either way — but it's worth stating plainly rather than letting the earlier framing's precision overstate what the default's margin actually is.

**This also undercuts Option B's premise, not just Option A's viability.** Loosening the gate from its default (rate 0.98) all the way to off (rate 0.0) moves the lease rate roughly 160× (0.00175 → 0.28387), but the absolute distance covered is still 7–14× short of the 2–4 band. If the shipped gate's specific threshold were the thing calibrating Operator's leasing frequency to "a rational, conservative actor" per D35's own reasoning for reading this row against Operator, removing that threshold entirely should have moved the needle far more than it did — it didn't. That doesn't prove what the dominant constraint actually is (the sweep only varied `ChokepointTrafficRate`/`ChokepointMinObserved`, nothing else), but it does show the gate `#204` identified isn't sufficient on its own to explain the size of the gap — a real, measured fact about the two tested endpoints, not an inference about which untested mechanism fills the rest. Recalibrating GDD §22's band to match the *shipped default's* measured range (Option B) would still be risky on that narrower basis alone: it would treat a two-point sweep as if it had characterized "Operator's calibrated leasing model" in full, which is exactly the risk `#231`'s own text flagged for B ("quietly validating an overly cautious Operator rather than checking whether the caution itself is warranted").

**Supplementary, lower-fidelity curve (not evidence for any single value, and not a monotonicity proof).** An earlier 1,000-match pass at five intermediate gate settings — without formal intervals, point estimates only — happened to come out increasing between the two endpoints above as the gate loosened (roughly 0.016, 0.098, 0.168, 0.255, 0.266 leases/player at `(0.90,6)`, `(0.80,5)`, `(0.70,4)`, `(0.60,3)`, `(0.50,3)` respectively), with the RP margin crossing from positive to negative somewhere around `(0.70,4)`–`(0.60,3)`. None of these five points is precise enough at n=1,000 to cite as a value, and the pattern isn't a substitute for the monotonicity proof the previous paragraph notes this decision doesn't have — it's shown only because the shape is consistent with the two D35-rigor endpoints this decision's conclusion actually rests on, not as evidence that no untested intermediate configuration could differ.

**Why C over reopening A with a different dial.** Having ruled out the two `OperatorOptions` fields `#204` and `#231` already named, one candidate worth checking next — untested by this decision, and not established here as *the* answer — is Operator's action-priority ordering: `chooseObjective` (`internal/bots/operator.go:205-212`) only ever considers a chokepoint objective in a round where `runnerObjective` finds nothing, and `pickOperatorAction` (`operator.go:578-596`) checks Deliver and Pickup ahead of StakePost unconditionally, so a bot mostly occupied chasing contracts may rarely reach the chokepoint branch regardless of how permissive the gate is. That's a plausible next hypothesis given where the gate-off sweep still fell short, not a diagnosed cause — nothing in this decision traced an actual `Decide` call to confirm it. Testing it is a materially larger change than a tuning-pair sweep besides, touching behavior the golden RP-margin test and `operator_test.go`'s own hand-constructed cases already cover, so it is not a decision to make unilaterally inside this document; it is its own task, scoped and reviewed like any other change to `internal/bots`, and only worth taking on if a human-play signal from M5.5 says the lease rate is actually a problem worth chasing. Filed as [#237](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/237) as a hypothesis to test, explicitly blocked on M5.5's read rather than opened now.

**Why C is procedurally sound despite `cinzal-implementation-plan.md`'s "exit criteria are numbers, not code."** That line (§2, P1) guards against skipping M2's *measurement*, not against a row whose measurement is complete and whose answer is "not reachable by simulation." The roadmap already has this shape precedented three ways: row 13 of GDD §22 "ships without a precise answer and says so" (`cinzal-implementation-plan.md`, M2 deliverables); rows 15/16/18 defer to M5/M5.5 per [D33](D33-telemetry-event-stream-coverage.md); and the M2 exit-criteria section's own closing caveat states a sweep "tells you the shape of the parameter space... not the exact value. It narrows the range that M5.5 then confirms." The lease-rate row has, in fact, been narrowed — from "unknown" to "not reachable by any bot-simulation lever tested, at any RP cost" — which is the shape of answer M2's own caveat anticipates for a row like this.

## Consequences

- **`game.Config` and `OperatorOptions` ship unchanged.** No PR retunes `LeaseCostPerBlock`, `LeaseBlockRounds`, `ChokepointTrafficRate`, or `ChokepointMinObserved` off this decision.
- **The lease-rate row of M2's exit-criteria table closes as "measured, out of band across the tested range including the gate's own removal, deferred to M5.5"** — not as "watch" (D35's watch state is for a straddling interval; every interval measured here, in both `#204` and this decision's own sweep, sits fully outside the band) and not as silently met.
- **GDD §22's 2–4/player band is not edited.** Per the Reasoning above, this decision does not have grounds to say what the right number is — only that neither the current bot simulation nor its most permissive reachable variant can answer that question, which is a different finding from "the band is wrong."
- **[#236](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/236)** (the `LeaseBlockRounds=1` renewal-timing gap) and **[#237](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/237)** (Operator's action-priority ordering as a candidate next constraint on stake attempts, untested here and blocked on M5.5) are filed as follow-up tasks, independent of this decision closing.
- **Reversible at low cost.** Nothing in `internal/rules` or shipped `internal/bots` tuning changes here; a future decision revisiting this one (e.g., after M5.5 human data) means re-running sweeps against a possibly-changed Operator, not unwinding a merged code change.
