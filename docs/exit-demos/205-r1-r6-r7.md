# Exit demo: R1, R6 and R7 have numbers at 2/3/4/5 players

**Issue:** [#205](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/205)
**Milestone:** M2 — Bots and simulation
**Method:** [D35](../decisions/D35-simulation-sample-size-and-verdict-rule.md) — 10,000 matches per configuration, `mean ± 1.96 · s / √n` over the per-match vector, action only when the whole interval sits on the failing side. Both root seeds are **pooled** into one 20,000-match vector before any verdict is read, per D35's own note that "#205 already does a second-seed re-run unconditionally for its three metrics; pooling still applies to that stricter case."

Raw CSVs for all 16 configurations (4 player counts × 2 tiers × 2 seeds) are in [`205/`](205/), two files each, produced by:

```bash
simulate --matches 10000 --players <N> --bots <drifter|operator> \
  --sweep Rounds=15 [--seed <root-seed-string>] \
  --out          205/p<N>-<tier>-<seed-label>.csv \
  --breakdown    205/p<N>-<tier>-<seed-label>-breakdown.csv
```

`--sweep` is a required flag (at least one dimension), so `Rounds=15` is used as a no-op dimension — 15 is already `DefaultConfig()`'s own value, so every run's `Config` is the unmodified shipped default. `--breakdown` is this demonstration's own addition to `cmd/simulate` and is what produces the per-round, per-card and Tier IV tables below; the `--out` file carries GDD §22's rows as always. The breakdown file's `r1_overall`, `r6_overall` and `r7_infamy9/final` rows are recomputations of §22 rows 1, 6 and 4, and reproduce them exactly. `cmd/simulate/breakdown_test.go` asserts this twice over: per match, that each `Breakdown`'s raw counts equal the corresponding `telemetry.MatchSummary` field; and after aggregation, that the three rows' mean, half-width, effective `n` and excluded count are identical to what `metricSpecs` produces from the same matches' summaries. The headline tables below and the `--out` CSVs therefore carry the same figures, and do.

**On reproducibility.** The whole 16-configuration sweep was run twice, from two different commits — once before this PR's review fixes and once after, the second time against a harness whose incident-deck peek had been refactored to share `internal/rules`' own. All 32 files came back **byte-identical apart from the `git_sha` field**, which is the determinism claim RFC §6.3 makes about `seed + order log`, exercised across a real refactor rather than a re-run of the same binary.

**On pooling arithmetic.** The pooled mean and interval are computed from each run's own `(mean, half_width, n)` triple rather than by re-running with a merged seed set — the two are algebraically identical (`s_i = h_i·√n_i/1.96`, then the usual pooled sum of squares), and the CSVs carry six decimal places, which is ~4 significant figures on every half-width reported here.

The denominator is printed because §22's wording is *"< 15% **of submitted routes**"*: it counts every order in the log whose `Route` is non-empty — the population `EventRouteHalted` could possibly have fired against. The per-match figure and the pooled ratio agree to within 0.15 percentage points everywhere (a match with fewer routes carries the same weight as one with more, by D35's own "the match is the sampling unit").

### Verdict: ACTION at every player count, on both tiers.

Operator clears the 15% line by **2.0× at 2 players and 3.8× at 5 players**, with half-widths of 0.18–0.21 pp against distances of 15.1–41.4 pp — a 73× to 236× margin. Drifter — not the verdict tier, but reported per D35 — clears it too, at every player count. Nothing here is close to a straddle; pooling changed no verdict, and neither seed's own 10,000-match interval came within 3 percentage points of 15% anywhere.

**The remedy §20 already names, recorded without amendment:** *"most likely by letting the loser continue their route from the fallback node with remaining steps intact."* Filed as [#245](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/245) (D39) — a GDD §15 rule change with a stated hedge (*"most likely by"*), coupled to §22 row 9's Evasive band, and with one prerequisite noted below.

> **Resolved by [D39](../decisions/D39-r1-confrontation-softening.md).** §20's named remedy is **not** the one adopted: implemented literally it has to retrace the loser through the node they were just pushed out of, and it leaves this row at 40.36% at 4 players. §15 instead keeps the loser's remaining *steps* as §9.1 blind steps from the fallback node, and extends the same to tie participants. D39 also re-specifies this row's numerator — see the note at the end of this section — so the figures in this demonstration are the last ones measured under the old count. **The verdict below is unchanged under both counts**; only its margin moves.

**It is not concentrated — it is the steady state of the game from round 3 onward.** Every round from 3 to 15 sits above the 15% line at every player count, and at 5 players every one of those rounds sits above 48%. Round 2 is the one conspicuous dip (8.75%–21.18%, roughly a third of the surrounding rounds) and **this demonstration does not establish why.** The plausible reading is that it is the first round in which a seat has any trail at all to steer away from, and the last one before contract origins and destinations start pulling everyone toward the same warehouses and borders — but nothing measured here tests that, and it is recorded as an observation rather than an explanation.

### Context, not a second verdict

Confrontations per match at the shipped default, pooled — Drifter 4.50 / 8.23 / 11.78 / 19.12 and Operator 6.56 / 12.49 / 18.03 / 26.31 at 2/3/4/5 players. R1's numerator is downstream of this: `EventRouteHalted` fires wherever a confrontation clears a seat's remaining route (a tie participant, a decisive loser, or the rare crossing-corrected winner), so a match with 18 confrontations produces roughly 26 halts against roughly 57 submitted routes. **R9's own remedy has already been spent at 4 players** ([#234](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/pull/234) raised it from 25 to 28 nodes). On the 25-node map, [#203](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/203)'s CSVs read 49.24% and 49.26% for this same cell; on the 28-node map it reads 45.70%. Three nodes bought 3.5 percentage points against a line 30 points below. This is not a number a further node-count change plausibly rescues, which is why §20's own R1 remedy is about the confrontation rule and not about the map.

`EventRouteHalted` carries no cause field, so this demonstration cannot split the numerator into decisive losses versus ties, and §20's named remedy only helps the former. That split is worth having before the remedy is designed — it is [#245](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/245)'s first step — and it does not change this verdict.

> **[D39](../decisions/D39-r1-confrontation-softening.md) took the split, and found more than a missing field.** The causes are ~70% decisive loser, ~21–24% tie participant, ~6–7% crossing-corrected winner, stable across player counts. It also found that this row's numerator counts halt *events* against a denominator of submitted *routes*: about 3% of the events land on seats that submitted no route, 6%–17% are a second catch on a route already cancelled, and ~28%–30% are catches on a plan that had already run out of steps. Counting **routes** instead, as the row's own wording requires, the same matches read **17.32% / 22.64% / 24.93% / 29.13%** (Operator, 2/3/4/5 players) — still failing everywhere, so the ACTION verdict above stands, but the margin is 1.16×–1.94× rather than the 2.0×–3.8× stated above. The one figure in this demonstration that a reader should not carry forward unqualified is that multiple.

### Verdict: NO ACTION at any player count — and the 2-player near-miss from the first cut is gone.

Both failing ends have to be reported, and both are clear: no interval comes near 70%, and every interval sits well above 20%. **Five of sixteen cards never register a hit, and none of the five can — as row 6 is instrumented.** Sinkhole, Informant Ring, Spilled Load, Open Doors and Word of Work resolve into a node made impassable, a public position reveal, a crate dropped on the ground, a discounted purchase, an extra contract offer — none of them a seat being hit. Sinkhole and Word of Work say so in their own doc comments (*"this card affects a node, not a player"*; *"a contract offer is a boon, not a hit"*). This is unchanged from the first cut, which correctly identified these five (of its seven zeros) as genuine, not instrumentation gaps.

**Gas Leak and Riot no longer read 0.0%, and neither is an ordinary card once corrected.** `EventIncidentHit` now has eleven resolvers reaching it — the nine inside `incidents.go` plus these two call sites — and both cards turn out to be far from the ~42%–75% band the other eight live cards cluster in:

- **Gas Leak is the single highest-rate card in the entire deck, by a wide margin: 63.4%–86.7%.** Its truncation fires the moment *any* movement step's destination lands in the flagged sector — not only a route that *ends* there, the condition every other hazard checks (`internal/rules/movement.go`'s `advance()`, unchanged by this fix; only the event it was already producing state-wise is now reported). A seat merely passing through the sector mid-route triggers it, which is a broader eligibility than "ended in the sector" and is why its rate clears every other card in the table, at every player count.
- **Riot is now the single lowest-rate live card: 13.2%–40.3%, below even Flood's own conditional low.** `EventIncidentHit` only fires for a Riot-permuted entry that already names a seat (`entry.Actor`/`entry.Target`, never `riotParticipant`'s deliberately ambiguous sort-key sentinel — see D40's own reasoning for why reusing that value would have minted false hits). A Fresh Tracks entry, or a cargo-taken/item-purchased entry below its own Infamy naming gate, contributes nothing, so Riot's rate tracks how often the sector's eligible trail entries happen to be named ones, not how often the sector is occupied at all.

**Torched is still a near-zero card, and still a different case from the five above: it can hit, and effectively doesn't.** It hits **lease owners**, and Operator almost never holds a lease — the same bottleneck [#204](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/204) measured and [D36](../decisions/D36-lease-rate-chokepoint-gate.md) deferred. 4, 2, 5 and 6 hits across roughly 16,350 matches each (0.003%–0.037%) — small numbers that moved from the first cut's 9/5/1/8 by ordinary match-to-match sensitivity at this sample size, not by anything this fix touched (Torched's own resolver is untouched). It stays with the lease-rate finding, not with the five zeros above.

**The aggregate is therefore the product of two independent things**, and only one of them is what R6 is asking about. The **live cards** are now **ten**, not eight: the original eight (Flood, Snatch Job, Guard Sweep, Turf War, Streets Blocked, Shakedown, Local Informant, Distracted Guard) plus Gas Leak and Riot, corrected onto the list they always belonged on — 8 of 11 hazards and 2 of 5 boons, excluding only the five genuine zeros and Torched. Measured, they accounted for **162,897 of 260,000 live incident rounds at 2 players, 62.65%**, and 62.64%–62.68% at every other player count (up from the first cut's ~50.06%, since two more named cards now count as live — the deck itself, and how often it deals any given card, has not changed). Conditional on the drawn card being one of the ten, the hit rate is:

| 2p | 3p | 4p | 5p |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40.98% | 52.66% | 62.69% | **71.07%** |

`0.6266 × 0.6269 = 0.3928`, which is the measured 4-player aggregate of 39.28% to four figures. This conditional rate barely moved from the first cut's 41.16%/52.75%/63.38%/71.82% (eight cards) — Gas Leak pulling it up and Riot pulling it down largely offset within the average, even though neither is anywhere near it individually. **That cancellation is a coincidence of this fix's two cards, not a reason to trust the conditional mean as representative:** eight of the ten live cards still cluster tightly (Flood the one consistent low outlier, as before, three to four points under the rest), but Gas Leak and Riot now sit 20+ points on either side of that cluster — the single conditional number hides a genuinely bimodal deck in a way it did not before this fix, and a reader who wants "how hard does the card that hit, hit" from this number alone is now further from the truth than the first cut's version of the same number was.

**At 5 players the conditional rate is still above §22's own 70% failing line, and at 4 players still above the 60% top of the target band** — 71.07% and 62.69%, both within a few tenths of the first cut's 71.82%/63.38%. Row 6 is not defined that way and this demonstration does not redefine it — the verdict above stands on the row as written. But a reader deciding whether incidents feel *"unavoidable"* is asking about the card that fired, not about the coin-flip chance that the drawn card could fire at all, and for that reader the conditional number is the one that answers the question — with the caveat immediately above about what it now hides.

### The dials, in §20's stated order, recorded because R6's third one asked to be named

No dial is turned — the threshold did not trip. §20's order is *"start at round 5; odd rounds only; cut the deck to the six least punishing."* The third one asked which six, and the first cut's own answer — *"it cannot be steered by hit rate: among the eight live cards, the rates are near-identical"* — **no longer holds now that Gas Leak and Riot are correctly counted.** Hit rate now spans 13.2% to 86.7% among the ten live cards, a wide enough range to rank by. But ranking by *hit rate* is still not the same question §20 is actually asking: a card that hits 63%–87% of the time by truncating a route mid-step (Gas Leak) and one that hits 13%–40% of the time by falsifying a trail entry's node (Riot) are not commensurable with a card that hits ~42%–75% of the time for a flat Cr$4 (Shakedown) on hit-rate alone — the dial needs a severity measure (cash lost, steps lost, cargo forfeited), and GDD §22 still has no row that carries one. **What this correction changes is that hit rate is no longer a reason to *rule out* using it as a partial signal** — it was flat before because two of the highest- and lowest-impact cards in the deck were misread as zero; now that they read correctly, hit rate at least separates Gas Leak and Riot from the rest, even without a severity measure to combine it with. If that dial is ever reached for, it still needs new instrumentation, not this table alone.

## Findings that are not verdicts

Four things this demonstration surfaced that its own three questions do not cover. None changes a verdict above; all four are filed.

1. **A zero-width interval is written as a measurement.** `cmd/simulate`'s `interval` treats `n < 2` as "no measurement" but not a constant vector, so a row where every match scored the same — Drifter's Tier IV deliveries, at every player count — is written as `0.000000 ± 0.000000` and reads like a precisely pinned value. The roadmap states the rule for both cases in one sentence (*"on the same reasoning as the zero-width interval below"*) and only the first half is implemented. → [#249](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/249). The affected cells are handled in prose above; the CSVs are not, which is the point of the issue.

2. ~~**Gas Leak and Riot are structurally invisible to §22 row 6, and Gas Leak is arguably the most punishing card in the deck.**~~ **Resolved.** Both cards resolved outside `incident()`, in an earlier phase, and neither emitted `EventIncidentHit`. [D33](../decisions/D33-telemetry-event-stream-coverage.md)'s row-6 resolver list named only the resolvers living inside `incidents.go`, so the implementation matched the decision exactly — the decision is what did not consider them. → [#246](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/246) (D40), implemented by [#274](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/274); the R6 section above is the corrected re-measurement, and confirms the second half of this finding — Gas Leak is now measurably the single highest-rate card in the entire deck, not merely "arguably."

3. ~~**Gas Leak also clears a seat's route and action without emitting any event at all**~~ **Resolved.** Sat awkwardly against GDD §15.0's *"orders never silently fail"* — every other Step 0 degradation produces a specific kind (`EventRouteTruncated`, `EventCurfewTruncated`, `EventDeliveryBlocked`, …), and a confrontation halting a route emits `EventRouteHalted`. → [#274](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/274) adds `EventGasLeakTruncated`, fired from the same branch as finding 2's fix.

4. **`rules.Contract.ExpiresRound`'s doc comment says it is "decremented by Upkeep step 1."** It is not — `upkeepContracts` compares `s.Round < c.ExpiresRound` against an absolute round, and nothing writes the field after `AcceptOffer` except the Deadline Pause, which extends it. A one-line documentation error, found while building the Tier IV contract tracking. → [#247](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/247).
