# Exit demo: R1, R6 and R7 have numbers at 2/3/4/5 players

**Issue:** [#205](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/205)
**Milestone:** M2 — Bots and simulation
**Method:** [D35](../decisions/D35-simulation-sample-size-and-verdict-rule.md) — 10,000 matches per configuration, `mean ± 1.96 · s / √n` over the per-match vector, action only when the whole interval sits on the failing side. Both root seeds are **pooled** into one 20,000-match vector before any verdict is read, per D35's own note that "#205 already does a second-seed re-run unconditionally for its three metrics; pooling still applies to that stricter case."

Raw CSVs for all 16 configurations (4 player counts × 2 tiers × 2 seeds) are in [`205/`](205/), two files each, produced by:

```bash
simulate --matches 10000 --players <N> --bots <drifter|operator> \
  --sweep Rounds=15 [--seed <root-seed-string>] \
  --out          205/p<N>-<tier>-<seed-label>.csv \
  --breakdown    205/p<N>-<tier>-<seed-label>-breakdown.csv
```

`--sweep` is a required flag (at least one dimension), so `Rounds=15` is used as a no-op dimension — 15 is already `DefaultConfig()`'s own value, so every run's `Config` is the unmodified shipped default. `--breakdown` is this demonstration's own addition to `cmd/simulate` and is what produces the per-round, per-card and Tier IV tables below; the `--out` file carries GDD §22's rows as always. The breakdown file's `r1_overall`, `r6_overall` and `r7_infamy9/final` rows are recomputations of §22 rows 1, 6 and 4, and reproduce them exactly. `cmd/simulate/breakdown_test.go` asserts this twice over: per match, that each `Breakdown`'s raw counts equal the corresponding `telemetry.MatchSummary` field; and after aggregation, that the three rows' mean, half-width, effective `n` and excluded count are identical to what `metricSpecs` produces from the same matches' summaries. The headline tables below and the `--out` CSVs therefore carry the same figures, and do.

**On reproducibility.** The whole 16-configuration sweep was run twice, from two different commits — once before this PR's review fixes and once after, the second time against a harness whose incident-deck peek had been refactored to share `internal/rules`' own. All 32 files came back **byte-identical apart from the `git_sha` field**, which is the determinism claim RFC §6.3 makes about `seed + order log`, exercised across a real refactor rather than a re-run of the same binary.

**On pooling arithmetic.** The pooled mean and interval are computed from each run's own `(mean, half_width, n)` triple rather than by re-running with a merged seed set — the two are algebraically identical (`s_i = h_i·√n_i/1.96`, then the usual pooled sum of squares), and the CSVs carry six decimal places, which is ~4 significant figures on every half-width reported here.

The denominator is printed because §22's wording is *"< 15% **of submitted routes**"*: it counts every order in the log whose `Route` is non-empty — the population `EventRouteHalted` could possibly have fired against. The per-match figure and the pooled ratio agree to within 0.15 percentage points everywhere (a match with fewer routes carries the same weight as one with more, by D35's own "the match is the sampling unit").

### Verdict: ACTION at every player count, on both tiers.

Operator clears the 15% line by **2.0× at 2 players and 3.8× at 5 players**, with half-widths of 0.18–0.21 pp against distances of 15.1–41.4 pp — a 73× to 236× margin. Drifter — not the verdict tier, but reported per D35 — clears it too, at every player count. Nothing here is close to a straddle; pooling changed no verdict, and neither seed's own 10,000-match interval came within 3 percentage points of 15% anywhere.

**The remedy §20 already names, recorded without amendment:** *"most likely by letting the loser continue their route from the fallback node with remaining steps intact."* Filed as [#245](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/245) (D39) — a GDD §15 rule change with a stated hedge (*"most likely by"*), coupled to §22 row 9's Evasive band, and with one prerequisite noted below.

**It is not concentrated — it is the steady state of the game from round 3 onward.** Every round from 3 to 15 sits above the 15% line at every player count, and at 5 players every one of those rounds sits above 48%. This is the *"reads as a lottery"* case, not the *"unlucky handful of rounds"* case, and it is what the distribution was asked for in order to distinguish.

Round 2 is the one conspicuous dip (8.75%–21.18%, roughly a third of the surrounding rounds) and **this demonstration does not establish why.** The plausible reading is that it is the first round in which a seat has any trail at all to steer away from, and the last one before contract origins and destinations start pulling everyone toward the same warehouses and borders — but nothing measured here tests that, and it is recorded as an observation rather than an explanation.

**And it is not a tail effect either.** The share of individual matches whose *own* rate clears 15% (Operator, pooled):

|---|---|---|---|

### Context, not a second verdict

Confrontations per match at the shipped default, pooled — Drifter 4.50 / 8.23 / 11.78 / 19.12 and Operator 6.56 / 12.49 / 18.03 / 26.31 at 2/3/4/5 players. R1's numerator is downstream of this: `EventRouteHalted` fires wherever a confrontation clears a seat's remaining route (a tie participant, a decisive loser, or the rare crossing-corrected winner), so a match with 18 confrontations produces roughly 26 halts against roughly 57 submitted routes. **R9's own remedy has already been spent at 4 players** ([#234](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/pull/234) raised it from 25 to 28 nodes). On the 25-node map, [#203](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/203)'s CSVs read 49.24% and 49.26% for this same cell; on the 28-node map it reads 45.70%. Three nodes bought 3.5 percentage points against a line 30 points below. This is not a number a further node-count change plausibly rescues, which is why §20's own R1 remedy is about the confrontation rule and not about the map.

`EventRouteHalted` carries no cause field, so this demonstration cannot split the numerator into decisive losses versus ties, and §20's named remedy only helps the former. That split is worth having before the remedy is designed — it is [#245](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/245)'s first step — and it does not change this verdict, which clears the line by 2–3.8× under every reading.

It is real and it is small.

**Seven of sixteen cards never register a hit, and none of the seven can — as row 6 is instrumented.** That is a statement about `EventIncidentHit`, not about whether the card does anything to a player, and the two halves come apart. `EventIncidentHit` has exactly nine resolvers (`internal/rules/incidents.go`).

- **Five of the seven genuinely do not hit anyone.** Sinkhole, Informant Ring, Spilled Load, Open Doors and Word of Work resolve into a node made impassable, a public position reveal, a crate dropped on the ground, a discounted purchase, an extra contract offer. Sinkhole and Word of Work say so in their own doc comments (*"this card affects a node, not a player"*; *"a contract offer is a boon, not a hit"*).
- **Gas Leak and Riot do affect the player, and are invisible to the row anyway.** Gas Leak cancels an eligible seat's move, route and action for the round; Riot falsifies the flagged sector's trail entries. Both resolve in an earlier phase than `incident()` and emit no `EventIncidentHit`, so they are zero here as a fact about the instrumentation rather than about the game. This is finding 2 below, and it is the reason the seven are not interchangeable.

**Torched is an eighth near-zero card, and a different case: it can hit, and effectively doesn't.** It hits **lease owners**, and Operator almost never holds a lease — the same bottleneck [#204](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/204) measured and [D36](../decisions/D36-lease-rate-chokepoint-gate.md) deferred. It is not a row-6 zero at any player count: 9, 5, 1 and 8 hits across roughly 16,350 matches each, or between 0.006% and 0.055%. Under Drifter, which stakes posts at random, the same card hits 11.8%–21.9% of the time — which confirms the mechanism rather than the card, and places Torched with the lease-rate finding rather than with the seven above.

**The aggregate is therefore the product of two independent things**, and only one of them is what R6 is asking about. Call the **eight live cards** the ones that actually land under Operator — Flood, Snatch Job, Guard Sweep, Turf War, Streets Blocked, Shakedown, Local Informant, Distracted Guard; that is 6 of 11 hazards and 2 of 5 boons, and it excludes both the seven that never register a hit and Torched. Drawn 9-and-4, they should be ≈ 6.5 of any 13-card deck; measured, they accounted for **130,147 of 260,000 live incident rounds at 2 players, 50.06%**, and the same to within 0.05 pp at every other player count. Conditional on the drawn card being one of the eight, the hit rate is:

| 2p | 3p | 4p | 5p |
|---|---|---|---|
| 41.16% | 52.75% | 63.38% | **71.82%** |

`0.5006 × 0.6338 = 0.3173`, which is the measured 4-player aggregate of 31.73% to four figures. Within each player count, seven of the eight live cards cluster inside about one interval width of each other (41.2%–42.0% at 2 players against a per-card half-width of roughly ±0.75 pp; 71.9%–72.8% at 5). **Flood is the one consistent outlier, three to four points low at every player count** — and it is the only one of the eight live cards whose hit is conditional on the seat's own state rather than on the seat merely being there: `resolveFlood` emits nothing unless the seat was carrying cargo or a retreat toward the sector edge existed.

**At 5 players the conditional rate is above §22's own 70% failing line, and at 4 players it is above the 60% top of the target band.** Row 6 is not defined that way and this demonstration does not redefine it — the verdict above stands on the row as written. But a reader deciding whether incidents feel *"unavoidable"* is asking about the card that fired, not about the coin-flip chance that the drawn card could fire at all, and for that reader the conditional number is the one that answers the question.

### The dials, in §20's stated order, recorded because R6's third one asked to be named

No dial is turned — the threshold did not trip. §20's order is *"start at round 5; odd rounds only; cut the deck to the six least punishing."* The third one asked which six, and the answer this table gives is that **it cannot be steered by hit rate**: among the eight live cards, the rates are near-identical, with only Flood separating out at all. Hit rate is overwhelmingly a property of *"was any seat standing in the flagged sector"*, which is the same question for every card. Ranking the deck by how punishing its cards are needs a severity measure — cash lost, steps lost, cargo forfeited — and GDD §22 has no row that carries one. If that dial is ever reached for, it needs new instrumentation, not this table.

### Verdict: NO ACTION at any player count. **2 players clears the failing line by 0.59 percentage points and misses the target band by nine.**

Every Operator interval sits entirely above 10%, so nothing trips. 3, 4 and 5 players also meet the `> 20%` target. 2 players does not: `[10.59%, 11.45%]` is above the failing line and less than a point clear of it, against a target of 20%. §22 defines no action there and this demonstration invents none.

**The Drifter/Operator gap points the right way, which is the first thing the issue asks to check.** *"A metric where a random bot reaches Infamy 9 more often than a planning one is a metric measuring the planner, not the gradient."* Operator reaches Infamy 9 between **4.9× and 13.3× more often** than Drifter at every player count. Whatever else is wrong here, the metric is not inverted, and the headline is not an artifact of Operator failing to climb.

### But the two things §20 says to actually watch both point the other way

**Watch item 1 — "Tier IV contracts being accepted and then abandoned."** Per match, pooled, Operator:

| Players | Offered | Accepted | Delivered | Abandoned (deadline missed) | Still held at final scoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.0350 | 0.0285 | 0.0013 | 0.0027 | 0.0245 |
| 3 | 0.3248 | 0.2365 | 0.0072 | 0.0380 | 0.1913 |
| 4 | 0.7623 | 0.5189 | 0.0087 | 0.1058 | 0.4043 |
| 5 | 1.2796 | 0.8733 | 0.0138 | 0.2188 | 0.6408 |

Tier IV's deadline is 6 rounds, so a contract accepted from round 10 onward reaches final scoring having neither been delivered nor missed — that column is large and it is not an error. (`Offered` carries one stated undercount: round 1's offer is staged before the first `Resolve` call and cleared by it, so no end-of-round observation sees it. Tier IV requires Infamy 9 and every seat starts at 0, so the undercount is structurally zero for this tier.) The rate that answers §20's question is therefore **abandoned ÷ (delivered + abandoned)**, over contracts that actually settled:

| Players | Operator | matches contributing | Drifter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 68.67% [58.35%, 78.99%] | 75 of 20,000 | 11 / 11 |
| 3 | 84.41% [82.02%, 86.81%] | 850 | 68 / 68 |
| 4 | **92.59% [91.49%, 93.68%]** | 2,129 | 221 / 221 |
| 5 | **94.07% [93.37%, 94.77%]** | 4,150 | 589 / 589 |

Most matches settle no Tier IV contract at all and are excluded from this row rather than scored as a zero (D35); the effective `n` is printed for that reason, and the 2-player interval is wide because it rests on 75 matches. The Drifter column is deliberately **not** given an interval: it delivered a Tier IV contract **zero times across 20,000 matches at every player count**, so every settled contract was abandoned and the per-match vector is a constant — the roadmap's own *"a zero-width interval is a degenerate sample, not a result."* It is reported as the raw count it is. (The CSVs do carry an interval on those cells, because `cmd/simulate` implements only the `n < 2` half of that rule — filed as [#249](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/249).)

At 4 and 5 players, **more than nine in ten Tier IV contracts that reach a resolution are missed**. Operator delivered 26 / 144 / 174 / 276 across the four player counts. This is §20's own arithmetic — *"roughly one round of slack"* against a 6-round deadline — showing up as a completion rate, and it is exactly the thing §20 asked to be watched for.

**Watch item 2 — "if nobody voluntarily crosses Infamy 8."** Every transition from below Infamy 9 to 9-or-above, classified by which of the seat's own Infamy-granting deliberate acts happened in the same round (coincidence, not causation — the same loose reading D33 settled on for §22 row 14). Per match, pooled, Operator:

| Players | Crossings | with a delivery | with a stake | with a confrontation win | **win and nothing else** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.2347 | 0.0445 | 0.0001 | 0.2089 | **0.1890 (80.5%)** |
| 3 | 0.6210 | 0.0735 | 0.0001 | 0.5753 | **0.5451 (87.8%)** |
| 4 | 0.9587 | 0.0750 | 0.0002 | 0.9161 | **0.8819 (92.0%)** |
| 5 | 1.6001 | 0.0954 | 0.0003 | 1.5488 | **1.5031 (93.9%)** |

**80–94% of Operator's crossings into Legend happen in a round whose only deliberate Infamy-granting act was winning a fight.** Deliveries coincide with 19% of crossings at 2 players and 6% at 5. Staking a first post in a sector — the other route up the ladder — coincides with essentially none, for the lease-rate reason already established. A residual 0.1–0.5% of crossings involve none of the three (a global event card's Infamy grant, or a seat re-crossing after a dip).

Reading the two together: Operator does not decline the climb — it accepts 68–82% of the Tier IV offers it is shown (571/701, 4,730/6,496, 10,378/15,246, 17,467/25,592 at 2/3/4/5 players) — but it arrives at Legend by fighting rather than by contracting, and once there it almost never completes the contract the climb exists to unlock. **The headline percentage is met, in the specific way the issue warned it could be met.**

§22 row 4 is a final-state read ([D33](../decisions/D33-telemetry-event-stream-coverage.md) row 4: `EventTierLegend` covers the whole 9–10 band, so only final Infamy answers it), which is the reading the verdict above uses. Measuring the literal wording — *any* seat at 9 or above at the end of *any* round — gives a much larger number:

|---|---|---|
| 2 | 11.02% | 19.55% [19.01%, 20.10%] |
| 3 | 23.72% | 46.41% [45.72%, 47.11%] |

| 5 | 43.74% | 82.94% [82.41%, 83.46%] |

Roughly **half of every visit to Legend does not survive to scoring** — Infamy falls on a lost confrontation, on Debt, and on upkeep decay. Both readings clear the 10% line at every player count, so the choice of reading changes no verdict here; it is recorded because a future reader comparing this against row 4 will otherwise see two different numbers for the same sentence.

### Standing caveat

## Findings that are not verdicts

Four things this demonstration surfaced that its own three questions do not cover. None changes a verdict above; all four are filed.

1. **A zero-width interval is written as a measurement.** `cmd/simulate`'s `interval` treats `n < 2` as "no measurement" but not a constant vector, so a row where every match scored the same — Drifter's Tier IV deliveries, at every player count — is written as `0.000000 ± 0.000000` and reads like a precisely pinned value. The roadmap states the rule for both cases in one sentence (*"on the same reasoning as the zero-width interval below"*) and only the first half is implemented. → [#249](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/249). The affected cells are handled in prose above; the CSVs are not, which is the point of the issue.

2. **Gas Leak and Riot are structurally invisible to §22 row 6, and Gas Leak is arguably the most punishing card in the deck.** Gas Leak zeroes an eligible seat's position change, route and action for the round (`internal/rules/movement.go`); Riot permutes the flagged sector's trail entries (`internal/rules/trail.go`). Both resolve outside `incident()`, in an earlier phase, and neither emits `EventIncidentHit`. [D33](../decisions/D33-telemetry-event-stream-coverage.md)'s row-6 resolver list names only the resolvers that live inside `incidents.go`, so the implementation matches the decision exactly — the decision is what did not consider them. Row 6 understates the incident load by two cards, one of which cancels a whole turn, and the 2-player cell clears its failing line by 0.47 points. → [#246](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/246) (D40).

3. **Gas Leak also clears a seat's route and action without emitting any event at all**, which sits awkwardly against GDD §15.0's *"orders never silently fail"* — every Step 0 degradation produces a specific kind (`EventRouteTruncated`, `EventCurfewTruncated`, `EventDeliveryBlocked`, …), and a confrontation halting a route emits `EventRouteHalted`. Related to finding 2 but a separate question: one is about telemetry, this one is about what the player is told. → carried on [#246](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/246), which decides whether it is one decision or two.

4. **`rules.Contract.ExpiresRound`'s doc comment says it is "decremented by Upkeep step 1."** It is not — `upkeepContracts` compares `s.Round < c.ExpiresRound` against an absolute round, and nothing writes the field after `AcceptOffer` except the Deadline Pause, which extends it. A one-line documentation error, found while building the Tier IV contract tracking. → [#247](https://github.com/garnizeh/cinzal/issues/247).
